## Supplementary Material

### 9.1 Errata and security-proof changelog

This source revision incorporates the following corrections and the zero-padded rectangular-row specialization. The specialization keeps the paper's joint polynomial, one challenge point, one SumCheck, and one terminal point. It pads the common row domain to a power of two and adds the canonical first matrix  $M_1=[I;0]$ . It does not add a column point, a second SumCheck, or a separate norm-opening object.

The selected specialization is modeled in [`PaddedRowIdentityV1.lean`](../../formal/nightstream-lean/Nightstream/Implementation/R1CS/Correspondence/FPrimeFullHistory/SelectiveCcs/PaddedRowIdentityV1.lean). Its model-level results prove that zero padding preserves and reflects the selected logical relation when  $f(0,\ldots,0)=0$ . Rust, transcript, codec, circuit, and production-artifact conformance remain separate implementation obligations.

| Area | Corrected convention or argument |
|---|---|
| Joint sum-check target | The carried target is encoded locally as  $T_{\mathrm{local}}$  and shifted to  $T_{\mathrm{abs}}=\gamma^{2K+k}T_{\mathrm{local}}$ . The combined sum-check uses  $T_{\mathrm{abs}}$ , so the fresh, norm, and carried constraints occupy disjoint exponent ranges. |
| Strict norm test and degree | The scalar norm polynomial is  $P_b(Z)=\prod_{a=-(b-1)}^{b-1}(Z-\iota_q(a))$ . For  $a\in\mathbb F$ ,  $P_b(a)=0$  if and only if  $|\operatorname{ctr}_q(a)|<b$ . Writing  $D_f=\deg_{\mathrm{tot}}f$ , the joint polynomial has individual degree at most  $D_Q=\max(D_f+1,2b,2)$ . |
| Universal ambient relation | The universal strict ambient bound is the integer  $B_{\mathrm{amb}}=\lfloor q/2\rfloor+1=(q+1)/2$ . Every centered field representative has magnitude strictly below this bound. The protocol and binding bounds remain  $B=b^k<q/2$ . |
| Coordinate-wise extraction | Theorem 10 loses  $\ell/|\mathcal C|$ , where  $|\mathcal C|$  is the per-coordinate challenge-set size. Appendix D.5 keeps the conservative corollary  $(\ell+1)/|\mathcal C|$ . |
| Strong extraction for  $\Pi_{\mathrm{CCS}}$ | The extractor now makes one unconditioned execution and outputs  $\perp$  if it is not ambient-successful. Only after that success does it repeat fresh executions until obtaining a second ambient-successful witness. This success-gated order uses at most two fresh protocol executions in expectation for every fixed extractor input. For fixed input  $x$ , witness disagreement costs  $\delta_x/p_x$ ; because  $\delta_x\le p_x^2$ , Cauchy--Schwarz gives the global loss  $\mathbb E_x[\delta_x/p_x]\le\sqrt{\delta}$ . Together with the direct joint-event sum-check and Schwartz--Zippel bounds, extraction succeeds with probability at least  $\epsilon'-\sqrt\delta-\overline\epsilon_{\mathrm{SC}}-\epsilon_{\mathrm{SZ}}=\epsilon'-\operatorname{negl}(\lambda)$ . No pointwise success-floor premise is required. |
| One-sided inner-product transform | The transform is applied to the matrix block only:  $\operatorname{ct}(\overline{\mathbf a}\mathbf b)=\langle a,b\rangle$ . Field multilinear extensions use a tilde and ring-valued multilinear extensions use a hat. The matrix-product and evaluation-homomorphism statements now have matching types. |
| Row domain and packed witness length | The power-of-two value  $m$  is the SumCheck row-domain length. The packed witness length is  $n_{\mathbb F}=dn_R\le m$ . The matrix  $M_1$  injects the witness into that domain and pads the remaining rows with zeros. The generic relation can repeat existing constraint rows. When  $f(0,\ldots,0)=0$ , the Nightstream specialization instead appends zero rows to the other matrices. This permits the degree-54 concrete rings without changing the paper's single joint SumCheck. Commitment setup uses ring-message length  $n_R$ . |
| Relation domains | Admissible structures include the canonical injection  $M_1=[I;0]$ , so the norm polynomial checks the norm of the relation witness. Batched evaluation relations use  $\operatorname{BatchCE}_N$ , which contains one shared evaluation point. The normalization statement is limited to norm-bounded CCS and records the changes to  $t$  and  $m$ . |
| Aborts and public coins | Consistency is stated as zero probability of a mismatch when both executions do not abort. Public-coin challenges are exact uniform elements of finite challenge sets, not fixed-length bit strings. |
| Composition, RLC, and decomposition | The strong--weak composition proof now preserves  $u_1$  in an explicit augmented state and lets the constructed prover interact with the external first verifier. Ring-linear combinations of public field inputs and witnesses in  $\Pi_{\mathrm{RLC}}$  are typed through coefficient embeddings and their inverses. The signed base- $b$  split is a total deterministic map with explicit failure, and it commutes with coordinate projection. |
| Concrete parameters and scripts | Appendix B distinguishes heuristic Module-SIS work-factor estimates from end-to-end statistical soundness and states the field and coordinate-fork terms separately. It gives the one-activation statistical terms for normalized R1CS with  $t=4$  and records the square-root binding loss. The concrete lengths use  $n_R=\lfloor m/d\rfloor$ . Appendix D.7 places the full analytic product under the square root; its corrected  $\log_2 B$  diagnostics are approximately 13.40, 14.39, and 14.44. Appendices D.7 and D.8 state their reproduction limits. |
| Cyclotomic hypotheses | Theorem 8 now requires  $z$  and  $\eta$  to have the same prime support. It cites the later proof of the singular-value bound used for even  $z$ . The D.7 search enforces the same condition. |
| Security scope | Every negligible-error claim is conditional on the explicit, efficiently constructible parameter-family convention in Section 4. The Appendix B tables are fixed snapshots and do not prove such a family. D1 and the comparison table classify the computational assumption only. The knowledge extractors are classical. Quantum-prover extraction and Fiat--Shamir security in the quantum random-oracle model are outside the claim. |
| Implementation conformance | The paper proves the single-domain interactive protocol. Rust sampler bounds, transcript encodings, Fiat--Shamir derivation, and Rust--Lean correspondence are separate implementation obligations. No theorem in this manuscript claims those properties. |
| Source provenance | The front matter identifies this manuscript as a locally corrected working copy and does not attribute the edited wording to the original authors. The single cumulative `superneo-paper-errata.patch` is generated from the pinned original ZIP by `docs/generate-superneo-errata-patch.sh`. |
| Source cleanup | The D.4 and D.5 strong/weak labels and the notation  $I(i,j,\ell)$  are made consistent. The CCS zero-set type, the domain of  $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{in}}$ , the RLC challenge range, Equation (16), and the missing Equation (6) label are corrected. |
| Post-review source corrections | The structure defines  $D_f$  separately from the invariant  $D_f\le u$ , and the mixed-radix map  $I$  is stated to be bijective. The CE and Module-SIS witness dimensions, the relaxed-binding commitment type, the expansion-factor domain, the Appendix B.3 extension-field type, and the introductory challenge-field wording are corrected. The success-gated runtime display is explicitly conditioned on entering the loop. The  $\Pi_{\mathrm{DEC}}$  completeness equations and transformed-matrix evaluation notation are corrected and made consistent. |

The old notation  $\mathrm{CE}(q/2,\mathcal L)$  mixed a rational-looking threshold with a relation parameter declared to lie in  $\mathbb N$ . The replacement  $B_{\mathrm{amb}}$  is the least integer bound for which every centered representative satisfies the paper's strict inequality, and avoids any ambiguity from interpreting division on natural numbers.
