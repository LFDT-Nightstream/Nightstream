## 2026-07-11 — compact sparse form storage rejected and reverted

- Hypothesis: since only 17,055 of 59,032 form blocks are touched, store only
  active form entries and propagate that compact device representation through
  Pi_CCS Y-eval and Pi_DEC. Expected value was roughly `60ms` of 8-chain active
  time while preserving the canonical dense proof surface at egress.
- Correctness gates before measurement: CUDA oxide build and `parity quick`
  passed, including DEC, full Pi_CCS proof, and NIFS proof byte equality.
- Profiled candidate:
  `gpuprof/20260711T-compact-forms-candidate-artifacts/` reports CPU aggregate
  `18072.44ms`, parallel CUDA `847.39ms`, and `21.33x`. Direct wall repeats were
  `835.13ms` and `842.58ms`, indistinguishable from the accepted `838.98ms`
  baseline.
- Kernel finding: the forms launch footprint fell about `3.46x` (118,064 total
  row/block slots to 34,110 active entries), but forms active union moved from
  about `87.6ms` to `95.2ms`. The zero blocks were cheap; the touched entries own
  the arithmetic. DEC's compact binary path saved only about `22ms` locally,
  and whole-window active union improved only `664.8ms -> 651.3ms`.
- Contract finding: repeated-loop D2H bytes and host joins did not decrease.
  This is therefore a local representation/kernel tweak, not the structural
  carrier win required for 25x.
- Verdict: rejected and fully reverted. Do not retry compact form storage unless
  a future profiler trace shows that memory bandwidth, rather than touched-entry
  arithmetic, has become the dominant form cost. Proceed with the device-backed
  Pi_CCS/Pi_RLC/Pi_DEC claim and proof carrier.

## 2026-07-11 — device fold-output/proof carrier retained

- Landed ownership boundary: Pi_CCS output surfaces/proof authority, Pi_RLC
  parent commitment/X/K surfaces, and Pi_DEC child commitment/X/K surfaces now
  remain device-backed. Ordinary `CeClaim`, `RunningInstance`, and `NifsProof`
  values are reconstructed at final proof/audit egress; canonical CE and full
  accumulator digests are checked during materialization.
- The first two digest implementations were rejected in place. V1 launched 128
  extra underfilled Poseidon hashes and profiled near `1112ms`; V2 fused those
  hashes but created 576 pinned host alloc/free calls and profiled near
  `1063ms`. V3 removed the pinned churn and restored wall time.
- V4 fused the three claim-preimage builders as well. Raw bundle
  `gpuprof/20260711T-device-claim-carrier-v4-artifacts/` reports parallel CUDA
  `889.63ms` under Nsight. Relative to the fresh control, builder launches are
  back to `96` from V3's `224`, D2H volume falls
  `29,813,760 -> 24,908,288` bytes, and `cuStreamSynchronize` calls fall
  `3712 -> 3504`. GPU active union is effectively flat
  (`664.78ms -> 668.30ms`), while the V4 gap is `184.92ms`.
- Clean non-Nsight direct repeats after the final restore include `847.84ms /
  20.91x`; build and `parity quick` pass with byte-identical proofs. A later
  back-to-back run (`987.83ms`, inflated CPU aggregate `25,465ms`) is rejected
  as a contaminated timing sample.
- Verdict: retain. It is wall-neutral against the accepted noise band but
  materially reduces repeated-loop transfer volume and host joins, and it
  finishes the ownership substrate needed by any future whole-GPU scheduler.
  The all-time accepted speed record remains `838.98ms / 21.20x`.

## 2026-07-11 — greedy ready-Poseidon scheduler rejected and reverted

- Profiler basis: in the V4 parallel window, 48
  `p2_hash_fields_cooperative_plan` launches sum `404.51ms` but overlap only
  `2.00x`. Each launch has just 15-16 blocks on the 128-SM RTX 4090. Nsight
  Compute measured one representative launch at `8.58ms`, `1.21%` compute
  throughput, and `2.08%` achieved occupancy, explicitly diagnosing a grid too
  small to fill the GPU.
- Candidate: one context-owned dispatcher greedily drained already-submitted
  jobs, used CUDA events for producer/consumer ordering, launched a combined
  raw-device-descriptor kernel, and scattered digests back D2D. It had no
  target-width wait or protocol-stage barrier.
- Result: the dispatcher formed 38 launches instead of 48, but 30 were still
  single-chain 15/16-block grids. Its descriptor-stream synchronization
  serialized useful work: active union rose to `758.78ms`; direct wall was
  `958.02ms / 18.53x`, and the raw Nsight bundle
  `gpuprof/20260711T-ready-poseidon-v1-artifacts/` reports `903.63ms / 19.96x`.
- Verdict: rejected and fully reverted. The underfilled Poseidon diagnosis is
  real, but a host dispatcher that waits while forming device descriptors is
  not the solution. A future retry needs asynchronous persistent descriptors
  or a device queue so ready jobs coalesce without adding a host serialization
  point.
